Coseismic Slip Models

Slip models of recent large earthquakes, inverted from space-geodetic measurements of the ground's displacement: L-band interferometry from NISAR and ALOS-2, Sentinel-1 interferograms and pixel offsets, and GNSS. Each model finds the distribution of slip on one or more fault surfaces in a layered elastic Earth.

All models use one inversion framework (SlipSolve-Python). The Green's functions are exact for triangular elements in a layered crust. The meshes are triangulations adapted to the resolution the data provide. The smoothing, and where tested the fault dips and depth extent, are chosen by spatially blocked cross-validation. Slip is bounded and solved exactly. NISAR data are processed from the single-look complex images with our own split-spectrum ionospheric correction (nisar_tools).

2026 Herrera Earthquake, Panama

Date
9 October 2026; Mw 7.7 (USGS)
Our model
Mw 7.68; peak slip 9.4 m, on the Azuero fault at about 8 km depth
Faults
Three sheets of the Azuero–Soná fault system (the main Azuero–Soná sheet, the Azuero fault and GEM fault 194), north-dipping, to 20 km depth, meeting at their junctions; left-lateral strike-slip
Data
NISAR, four tracks (two ascending, two descending), processed from SLCs with our ionospheric correction
The data: interferograms, GNSS and pixel offsets
Fits to the data and uncertainty

2026 Venezuela Earthquake

Date
24 June 2026; one rupture in two stages, which USGS lists as Mww 7.2 and Mww 7.5, 33 seconds apart (Mw 7.6 combined)
Our model
Mw 7.60 for the whole rupture (every image spans both stages); peak slip 3.4 m
Fault
The San Sebastián fault, right-lateral strike-slip, about 240 km long, vertical, to 35 km depth, with a softer north block above 25 km (rigidity ratio 1.6)
Data
NISAR, four tracks, processed from SLCs with our ionospheric correction; ALOS-2, three tracks
The data: interferograms, GNSS and pixel offsets
Fits to the data and uncertainty

2023 Kahramanmaraş Earthquake Doublet, Türkiye

Date
6 February 2023; Mw 7.8 (01:17 UTC) and Mw 7.7 (10:24 UTC)
Our model
Mw 7.84 and 7.69; peak slip 8.0 m (Pazarcık segment) and 8.4 m (Çardak fault)
Faults
The East Anatolian Fault (Amanos, Pazarcık and Erkenek segments, vertical), the Narlı fault, the Çardak fault (dipping 65° north) and a north–south strand, to 26 km depth; the dips and depth chosen by cross-validation
Data
Sentinel-1 range and azimuth offsets on four tracks (Ou et al., 2024), Sentinel-1 interferograms on five tracks in the far field (COMET LiCSAR), and GNSS for each event separately (Nevada Geodetic Laboratory)
The data: interferograms, GNSS and pixel offsets
Fits to the data and uncertainty

These models are research products and will change as the data and methods improve. Last updated 1 November 2026.

Data: NISAR (NASA/ISRO) through the Alaska Satellite Facility; ALOS-2 (JAXA); Copernicus Sentinel-1 (ESA), with interferograms from COMET LiCSAR and offsets from Ou, Lazecký, Watson, Maghsoudi and Wright (2024, CEDA); GNSS from the Nevada Geodetic Laboratory; earthquake parameters from the USGS. Maps: shaded relief from GMT's earth_relief (SRTM15+ and SRTM), coastlines and borders from GSHHG.
