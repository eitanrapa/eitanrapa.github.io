Coseismic Slip Models

Slip models of recent large earthquakes, inverted from space-geodetic measurements of the ground's displacement: L-band interferometry from NISAR and ALOS-2, Sentinel-1 interferograms and pixel offsets, Sentinel-2 optical offsets, and GNSS. Each model finds the distribution of slip on one or more fault surfaces in a layered elastic Earth.

All models use one inversion framework (SlipSolve-Python). The Green's functions are exact for triangular elements in a layered crust. The meshes are triangulations adapted to the resolution the data provide. The smoothing, and where tested the fault dips and depth extent, are chosen by spatially blocked cross-validation. Slip is bounded and solved exactly. NISAR data are processed from the single-look complex images with our own split-spectrum ionospheric correction (nisar_tools). Each model can be downloaded: the nodes and triangles of its mesh with their slip, as CSV.

2026 Kumamoto Earthquake, Japan

Date
28 July 2026, 07:27 UTC; Mw 6.8 (USGS)
Our model
Mw 6.84, peak slip 2.8 m at 5 km depth; right-lateral, with normal slip along the south-western two-thirds (rake −168°). It covers the earthquake and about three weeks after it, since the interferograms end 16–24 days later. Its moment is 1.34 times USGS's. GNSS grew by only 1–14% between days 1–5 and days 16–24, which does not account for that, though afterslip in the first days cannot be separated from the earthquake
Fault
The Hinagu fault zone on the mapped surface displacement boundary of the Geospatial Information Authority of Japan (GSI), dipping north-west: 70° at the surface shallowing to 50° at 20 km along its south-western half, and steeper to the north-east. The dips were chosen by cross-validation, which halves the held-out misfit against a vertical fault and prefers about 65° to USGS's 75°. Cross-validation asks for no extension past the mapped ends, under the Yatsushiro Sea, though about 1 m of slip at depth reaches the south-western end, so the rupture may not stop exactly there. The bottom at 20 km is a modelling choice because cross-validation is flat from 14 to 40 km: Mw 6.82–6.92 for bottoms at 14–28 km, 6.98 at 40 km
Data
NISAR on tracks 024 and 125 (ascending) and 003 and 104 (descending), processed from the radar images with our ionospheric correction, and 131 GNSS stations (Nevada Geodetic Laboratory) over days 16–24 to match the pairs. GSI's 3-D displacement field (GSI, 2026) is held out as a test. NISAR's azimuth offsets were tested and left out: they carry streaks of up to 0.75 m that run past the rupture's ends, and they improved nothing

2025 Mandalay Earthquake, Myanmar

Date
28 March 2025, 06:20 UTC; Mw 7.7
Our model
Mw 7.88, with the harmonic mean of the two sides' rigidities above 10 km (7.90 with the east block's alone); peak slip 5.6 m, near Mandalay and the epicentre
Fault
The Sagaing fault over 504 km, vertical in the south and dipping east to 60° in the north, to 30 km depth; the dips and depth chosen by cross-validation. The block west of the fault is 1.3 times softer above 10 km, as seismic velocities across the fault indicate (Xu et al., 2025), with the contrast solved exactly
Data
Two ALOS-2 interferograms, and range and azimuth offsets on four Sentinel-1 tracks and one LuTan-1 track (Xu et al., 2025)

2025 Dingri (Tingri) Earthquake, Tibet

Date
7 January 2025, 01:05 UTC; Mw 7.1 (USGS)
Our model
Mw 7.06: 7.05 on the Dengmocuo fault and 5.88 on the western graben fault; peak slip 3.0 m, at the surface on the Dengmocuo fault near 28.75°N
Faults
The west-dipping Dengmocuo fault, normal with a left-lateral part, on the trace its near-field step follows (the USGS-mapped breaks, continued along the interferograms and offsets), dipping 50° at the surface and flattening toward 35° at 25 km; and an antithetic fault on the graben's west side, dipping 45° east with normal slip only and ending on the main fault. Both to 20 km depth; the dips chosen by cross-validation. The bottom is a modelling choice: Mw about 7.02–7.09 for bottoms at 15–30 km
Data
Sentinel-1 interferograms on tracks 012 (ascending) and 121 (descending), ALOS-2 path 47's interferogram and azimuth offsets (He, Feng and Yun, 2025), and 12 GNSS stations (Li et al., 2025)

2024 Noto Peninsula Earthquake, Japan

Date
1 January 2024, 07:10 UTC; Mw 7.5 (USGS)
Our model
Mw 7.59, peak slip 7.7 m at the surface off the peninsula's north-western corner, with 5.7 m near 137.1° E and 4–5 m offshore to the north-east; reverse with a right-lateral part (rake 117°, against 102.5° in USGS's W-phase solution). Its moment is 1.35 times USGS's, partly from the layered crust's rigidity (mean 35 GPa; Mw 7.55 at a uniform 30 GPa). The swarm before the earthquake does not appear to explain the excess: two of the three pairs span months of it, and leaving both out raises Mw, to 7.62. Of the potency, 74% is above 10 km. All the data lie on the peninsula, on the hanging-wall side, but planted tests show they need the shallow and offshore slip: with no slip above 5 km in the truth, the inversion recovers 37% of the potency the real data give there, and with none north-east of the peninsula's tip, 12%. The split between 0–5 and 5–10 km is good to about 20%
Fault
A single fault along the north coast, on Enomoto and Kaneko's (2025) offshore segments stitched into a 136 km line and continued 5 km past each end, dipping south-east at 50° at the surface and shallowing to 30° at 20 km. We found no public file of the offshore trace. Cross-validation prefers this line to the straighter one of Guo et al. (2025), with a held-out misfit of 1.76 against 2.41, and asks for no extension past either end. One dip along strike does as well as any per-segment mix; a plain 45° is within 2%, and the two north-eastern segments, offshore of the data, do not constrain the dip. The bottom is at 20 km: cross-validation has a minimum at 18–20 km, and synthetic tests with true bottoms at 18 and 24 km both read 20, so it could lie from about 16 to 24 km (Mw 7.59–7.61); at 30 km Mw is 7.67. The separate western and eastern faults of Guo et al. (2025) have not been tested
Data
ALOS-2 range offsets on tracks 121 and 127 (ascending) and 026 (descending), from Xu et al. (2024); ALOS-2 interferograms of the same pairs, from Guo et al. (2025), only beyond 15 km of the line (25 km on track 026), because nearer in they read below the offsets by a cycle or more; and 365 GNSS stations (Nevada Geodetic Laboratory) over days 1–5. Stations near the fault differ from the offsets around them by 10–30% of their motion, so each station's sigma is at least 10% of its motion. ALOS-2 azimuth offsets were tested and left out: one track carries a 2 m step that no ground motion makes. So was Sentinel-1, whose coherence on the peninsula is about 0.1

2021 Maduo Earthquake, Qinghai, China

Date
21 May 2021, 18:04 UTC; Mw 7.3 (USGS)
Our model
Mw 7.45: 7.43 on the Kunlun Pass–Jiangcuo fault and 6.71 on its eastern branch; peak slip 4.7 m, at the surface on the main fault near 99.0°E. In a local crust (CSRM-1.0) the slip moves 0.13 m rms and Mw is 7.43
Faults
The Kunlun Pass–Jiangcuo fault on Hu's (2025) mapped trace, left-lateral, vertical at its western end and dipping 75–85° north along the rest, and a 35 km branch south of its eastern end dipping 80° south. Both to 25 km depth; the dips chosen by cross-validation. The bottom is a modelling choice, made on the aftershocks and the seismic moment because cross-validation cannot read it here: Mw 7.43–7.47 for bottoms at 20–30 km
Data
Sentinel-1 interferograms on tracks 026, 099 and 172 (ascending) and 033 and 106 (descending) from LiCSAR, and 004 (descending) from He et al. (2021); 106 GNSS stations (Wang et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2024). Optical offsets are held out as a test

Date
13 November 2016, 11:02 UTC; Mw 7.8 (USGS)
Our model
Mw 7.87; peak slip 12.5 m on the Kekerengu fault at 14 km depth
Faults
Six fault sheets after Xu et al. (2018), to 25 km depth: the Jordan–Kekerengu–Needles line dipping north-west (its north-eastern part at 70° near the surface, flattening to 20° at 25 km; its south-western part at 45°), the Stone Jug fault dipping 30° east-north-east, and the Papatea, Hundalee, North Leader and Humps faults vertical; the dips and depth chosen by cross-validation
Data
ALOS-2 interferograms on two tracks and a Sentinel-1 interferogram (Tong, Xu, Sandwell and colleagues, UC San Diego), GNSS (Nevada Geodetic Laboratory), and the near-field three-dimensional displacement field of Xu et al. (2026), weighted at its own residual so that it acts mainly as a check
